Eccentricity, cam and follower

Eccentricity

A disc is eccentric when it turns about a point O different from its centre C. The eccentricity e is the distance OC. As it turns, C describes a circle of radius e: the disc's edge moves away from and back towards the axis, producing a reciprocating motion.

In Sikax: Circle tool (key D) to draw the disc, then a revolute joint on another point of the disc: the distance between that point and the centre is the eccentricity, shown in the body's properties.

Cam and follower

A cam turns a rotation into a translation (or oscillation) of the follower resting on it. With an eccentric circular cam and a follower guided along an axis through O:

stroke = 2·e y(θ) = e·sin θ + √((R + ρ)² − e²·cos² θ)

R is the cam radius, ρ the roller radius (ρ = 0 for a knife-edge).

Follower types

  • Knife-edge: contact at a point, the tip stays on the profile (|CP| = R). Simple, but wears fast.
  • Roller: a small wheel of radius ρ rolls on the cam (|CP| = R + ρ). Less friction.
  • Flat face: a plane face stays tangent to the cam. For an eccentric, the lift is exactly sinusoidal: y = e·sin θ + R.

In Sikax the contact slides freely: the cam can make full turns without blocking the computation.

Normal force and lift-off

The cam pushes the follower along the contact normal, which passes through the circle centre C. A real contact can only push: if the normal force becomes negative, the follower lifts off. A stiff enough return spring is then needed; Sikax reports when lift-off happens.
