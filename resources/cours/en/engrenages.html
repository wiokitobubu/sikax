Gears

A gear transmits rotation from one shaft to another through meshing teeth, without slipping: the speed ratio is exact and constant.

Three kinds of gears

  • External: two wheels with external teeth; they turn in opposite directions.
  • Internal: a pinion turns inside a ring with internal teeth; same direction, shorter centre distance.
  • Rack and pinion: a wheel and a toothed bar; turns a rotation into a translation.

Module and dimensions

The module m (mm) sets the size of the teeth. Two wheels only mesh if they have the same module (and the same pressure angle).

QuantityFormula
Pitch diameterd = m · Z
Pitch (on the pitch circle)p = π · m
Tip diameterda = d + 2·m
Root diameterdf = d − 2.5·m
Tooth heighth = 2.25·m

With α = 20° and standard teeth, at least 17 teeth are needed to avoid interference (uncorrected cutting).

Centre distance

external: a = (d₁ + d₂)/2 = m·(Z₁ + Z₂)/2 internal: a = (d₂ − d₁)/2 = m·(Z₂ − Z₁)/2

For a rack, the pinion axis is d/2 from the pitch line. Sikax compares the diagram's centre distance with the theoretical one and offers to adjust it.

Transmission ratio

The pitch circles roll without slipping: r₁·ω₁ = ∓ r₂·ω₂, hence

r = ω₂/ω₁ = − Z₁/Z₂ (external) r = + Z₁/Z₂ (internal) v = (m·Z/2)·ω (rack)

For a train of several gears:

r = (−1)n · (product of driving Z) / (product of driven Z) n: number of external contacts

An idler wheel changes the direction but not the ratio. For a planetary train (carrier C), Willis' formula reads, between sun 1 and ring 3:

(ω₁ − ωC) / (ω₃ − ωC) = − Z₃/Z₁

Tooth forces

The contact force Fn acts along the line of action, inclined by the pressure angle α (usually 20°) to the common tangent of the pitch circles. It is split at the pitch point I:

Ft = C₁ / r₁ = 2·C₁ / d₁ Fr = Ft · tan α Fn = Ft / cos α

  • Ft, tangential force: it transmits the torque.
  • Fr, radial force: it pushes the wheels apart and loads their bearings.

The wheels' revolute joints carry the whole Fn: this is what Sikax shows in their torsors.

Transmitted torques and efficiency

P = C · ω η = Pout / Pin C₂ = η · C₁ · Z₂/Z₁

The efficiency η (often 0.95 to 0.99 for a spur gear) accounts for tooth friction: the driven wheel receives η times the power delivered. For several stages, efficiencies multiply: ηtotal = η₁·η₂·…
