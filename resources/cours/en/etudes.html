Kinematics, statics and dynamics

Sikax's three studies answer three different questions about the same mechanism. They need different data and give different results.

Kinematics: the motion is imposed

Kinematics studies motion regardless of its causes. A motor imposes the angle of a revolute joint or the displacement of a prismatic joint as a function of time; Sikax deduces the position of every body, then velocities and accelerations by differentiating the joints' closure equations:

Φ(q, t) = 0 ⇒ Φq · q̇ = −Φt ⇒ Φq · q̈ = γ

When “inertia effects” is checked, Sikax then computes the forces needed to produce this motion: this is inverse dynamics. The motor torque obtained is the one a real motor would have to deliver.

You need as many motors as mobilities: a slider-crank (mobility 1) is driven by a single motor.

Statics: equilibrium

A body (or a set of bodies) is in equilibrium when the sum of the mechanical actions on it is zero:

Σ Fext = 0 and Σ MA(Fext) = 0 (at any point A)

In the plane this gives three equations per body (two for the resultant, one for the moment). The unknowns are the joint force components. If the mechanism is isostatic, there are as many equations as unknowns: the solution is unique. If it is hyperstatic, some unknowns stay undetermined (see Isostatism).

Dynamics: Newton's second law

For a body of mass m, centre of gravity G and moment of inertia JG about (G, z):

Σ Fext = m · aG Σ MG(Fext) = JG · θ̈

The dynamic study computes the motion from the forces: given a starting position and velocities, Sikax integrates these equations step by step in time (Radau method, see How Sikax computes). It is the only study where inertia carries the mechanism through dead points, like a flywheel in an engine.

In Sikax: Study panel → Kind of study. A pendulum, a mass on a spring, a mechanism released under gravity are dynamics; a mechanism driven by a motor at imposed speed is kinematics.
