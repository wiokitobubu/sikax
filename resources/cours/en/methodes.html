How Sikax computes

This sheet describes, for the curious, what happens when you click “Run”.

The equations

Each moving body is described by three coordinates q = (xG, yG, θ). Each joint is written as constraint equations Φ(q, t) = 0; joint forces are the Lagrange multipliers λ:

M · q̈ + Φqᵀ · λ = Q(q, q̇, t) Φ(q, t) = 0

Positions

The position satisfying the joints is found by the Gauss-Newton method, starting from the previous position (continuation). Near a dead point the prediction extends the positions already computed, to cross the dead point in the direction of motion.

Dynamic study: the integrator

The motion is obtained by integrating the equations in time with scipy.integrate.solve_ivp. Several methods are offered:

MethodTypeWhen to use it
Radau (default)implicit, order 5robust and accurate; stiff springs, strong damping, light bodies
BDFimplicit, variable orderstiff problems, often faster than Radau
LSODAautomaticswitches by itself between an explicit method and BDF; very fast
DOP853, RK45explicit (Runge-Kutta)smooth motions without stiffness

To keep these integrators stable everywhere, even at dead points, Sikax computes accelerations with an augmented Lagrangian (the joints are weighted by a very large coefficient α, and the exact solution is recovered by iterations) and makes the joints attractive: a small numerical drift fades by itself. At output times, the position is projected exactly back onto the joints.

Precision

The precision is the integrator's relative tolerance (10⁻⁸ by default): the error made at each step stays of that order. A looser precision (10⁻⁶) computes faster; a stricter one brings nothing beyond 10⁻¹⁰. The mechanical energy of a frictionless system is a good witness: it must stay constant.

Runaway

If a constant motor torque is balanced by nothing, the speed grows without end and the forces with it (they grow with the square of the speed). This is not a computing error: Sikax warns, and stops the computation beyond 10,000 rad/s.
