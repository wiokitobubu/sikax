Moment of inertia

The moment of inertia measures a body's resistance to being set into rotation, as mass measures its resistance to translation.

Definition

JΔ = ∫ r² dm (r: distance to the axis Δ; unit kg·m²)

Sikax asks for JG, the moment of inertia about the axis (G, z) perpendicular to the plane.

Parallel-axis theorem (Huygens)

About an axis parallel to (G, z), at a distance d from G:

JA = JG + m · d²

Role in the computations

The moment equation gives Σ MG = JG·θ̈: for the same torque, a flywheel (large J) accelerates little. In dynamics, inertia carries a mechanism through dead points; in kinematics with inertia effects, it increases the motor torque during accelerations.
