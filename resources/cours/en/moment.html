Moment of a force

The moment of a force measures its ability to make a body turn about a point.

Definition

For a force F applied at point P, its moment at point A is the vector:

MA(F) = AP ∧ F

In the (x, y) plane this vector lies along z; only its component is kept:

MA = (xP − xA) · Fy − (yP − yA) · Fx

  • Positive: the force turns anticlockwise.
  • Negative: clockwise.
  • Unit: the newton-metre (N·m).

Lever arm

The moment only depends on the distance d between A and the force's line of action (the lever arm):

|MA(F)| = F · d

A force whose line of action passes through A has zero moment at A: it does not turn about A. This is what happens at a slider-crank's dead point.

Forces in Sikax

A force is given by its components Fx, Fy (N), in the global frame or in the body frame (a “follower” force turning with the body). Components accept an expression of time, for example 50*sin(2*pi*t).

In Sikax: Force tool (key F): click the point of application and drag in the force's direction.
