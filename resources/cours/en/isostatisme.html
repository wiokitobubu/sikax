Isostatism and hyperstatism

Definition

A mechanism is isostatic when the equilibrium equations are enough to compute every joint force. If more unknowns than independent equations remain, it is hyperstatic; the number of extra unknowns is the degree of hyperstatism h.

Formula

h = Is − Es + m

  • Is: sum of the joints' static unknowns;
  • Es: number of equilibrium equations, 3·(N − 1) in the plane, 6·(N − 1) in space;
  • m: mobility.

Equivalent kinematic approach: h = Ec − rc, where Ec = 3γ (6γ in space) is the number of closure equations of the γ loops and rc their rank.

Consequences

  • h = 0: forces determined by statics; the assembly tolerates manufacturing errors.
  • h > 0: the force distribution depends on clearances, deformations and tolerances. Stiffer, but needs precise assembly. Sikax then gives the minimum-norm solution and says so.

Plane or space?

A planar diagram hides constraints: a drawn hinge may be a revolute joint (5 unknowns in space), a ball joint (3) or a sphere-cylinder joint (2). A planar four-bar linkage, isostatic in the plane, is hyperstatic of order 3 in space with four revolute joints. Each joint's spatial model, in its properties, lets the Validation tab do the real analysis.

Example: a straight spur gear between two perfect revolute joints is hyperstatic of order 1 in space (line contact); crowned teeth (point contact) make it isostatic.
