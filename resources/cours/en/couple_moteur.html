Motor torque and power

Torque

A couple (torque) is a mechanical action with zero resultant but a non-zero moment: it turns without pushing. Two equal and opposite forces with parallel lines of action a distance d apart form a couple C = F · d. Its moment is the same at every point.

Unit: N·m. In Sikax, the Torque tool (key C) applies a torque Cz to a body; it may depend on time.

Motor with imposed motion

A Sikax motor imposes the angle of a revolute joint (rotary motor) or the displacement of a prismatic joint (actuator, linear motor) following a motion law: constant speed, sine, trapezoidal speed profile, free expression… The kinematic study then gives the motor torque (or force) needed at each instant.

θ(t) = θ₀ + ω·t (constant speed, ω in rad/s; N in rpm = 30·ω/π)

Computed motor torque

The motor torque is the action of the motor (fixed on body A) on body B of the joint. It balances loads, gravity and inertia effects (inverse dynamics). Its sign tells whether the motor drives (same sign as the speed) or brakes (opposite sign).

Power

P = C · ω (rotation) P = F · v (translation) unit: watt (W)

Without friction, the motor's power changes the mechanism's energy: P = dE/dt. With gears of efficiency η, the output receives η times the input power.

A constant motor torque with no resisting load accelerates the mechanism endlessly: speed and forces grow without bound. Sikax warns about it (“runaway”).
