Torsor of a mechanical action

A torsor gathers in one object everything about a mechanical action: what it “pushes” and what it “turns”.

Definition

The torsor of a mechanical action at point A is the pair of vectors:

{T} = { R ; MA } R: resultant (N) MA: moment at A (N·m)

In the plane it reduces to three numbers: X, Y (components of R) and N (moment about z).

Change of point (Varignon's relation)

MB = MA + BA ∧ R

The resultant does not depend on the point; the moment does. A slider (glisseur) is a torsor with a point where the moment vanishes (a single force); a couple is a torsor with zero resultant.

Torsor transmitted by a joint

A perfect joint only transmits the components that prevent a motion. Sikax gives the torsor of the action of A on B, reduced at the joint point:

Planar jointMobilitiesStatic unknowns
FixednoneX, Y, N (3)

Point contactTx and RzY, normal force (1)
Gearrolling at the pitch pointFt, tangential force (1)

A joint's number of static unknowns is 3 minus its number of mobilities (in the plane; 6 minus its mobilities in space).

In Sikax: after a computation, select a joint: its torsor appears in the properties, and the Forces tab lists them all.
