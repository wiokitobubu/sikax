Centre of gravity

Definition

The centre of gravity G of a body is the point where its weight can be considered to act. It is the barycentre of its mass elements:

m · OG = Σ mi · OPi (or ∫ OP dm for a continuous body)

For a uniform body, G is the geometric centre: middle of a bar, centre of a disc.

Mass

The mass m (kg) appears in the weight P = m·g and in the dynamic resultant m·aG. A massless body is accepted in kinematics and statics; in dynamics every free motion must carry some inertia.

Position of G in Sikax

Gx, Gy are given in the body frame. The “Spread the mass over the bars” button places G at the barycentre of the bars, weighted by their length; “Uniform disc” places it at the circle centre.

Set of bodies

xG = (m₁·x₁ + m₂·x₂ + …) / (m₁ + m₂ + …)

An unbalance appears when G is off the rotation axis: the centrifugal force m·e·ω² (e: distance from G to the axis) then loads the bearings, at the rotation frequency.
