Dead points

Definition

A dead point is a position where the driving member has lost its grip on the mechanism: a small change of the input produces no motion of the other member, or an infinite speed would be needed to follow it.

  • Slider-crank: at top and bottom dead centre, crank and connecting rod are aligned. The piston force passes through the crank axis: its moment is zero.
  • Four-bar linkage: when the rocker reaches an extreme position, crank and coupler are aligned.

How to get past them

  • Drive the member that keeps control (the crank rather than the piston).
  • Use inertia: a flywheel carries the mechanism past the dead point (combustion engine, treadle sewing machine). This is a dynamic study.
  • Combine several out-of-phase mechanisms (multi-cylinder engine).

Transmission angle

For a four-bar linkage, the transmission angle μ is the angle between coupler and rocker. Transmission is good for μ near 90°; it becomes poor (very large forces for a given torque) as μ approaches 0° or 180°, that is near a dead point.

What Sikax does

  • In kinematics, the mechanism is followed through dead points when the motor law allows it (piston driven over its whole stroke, with zero speed at the ends): the crank carries on in the direction of motion. Velocities and accelerations there come from the positions, and forces stay bounded.
  • If the law asks for the impossible (pushing the piston past its stroke), the computation stops cleanly and explains why.
  • Intervals spent near a dead point are shaded in the curves; a motor effort that shoots up there is reported.
